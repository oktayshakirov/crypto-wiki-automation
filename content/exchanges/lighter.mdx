Key components and features include:

- **Spot Trading:** Access to majors and long-tail **[altcoins](/posts/what-are-altcoins)**, subject to chain availability and liquidity. Slippage controls and route visualization help experienced traders manage execution risk.
- **Perpetual Futures (if supported in your region):** Lighter’s roadmap and product suite lean into derivatives that feel CEX-like but settle on-chain. While leverage can amplify returns, it also increases potential losses—use with caution and ensure collateralization is adequate.

## Security Measures
Security on Lighter begins with self-custody: you control your keys and authorize every transaction. That reduces counterparty risk inherent to custodial models, but introduces smart contract and wallet-security considerations.

What Lighter emphasizes:
- **Self-Custody by Design:** Funds remain in your connected wallet(s) unless deployed to smart contracts for open positions, LPing, or collateralization. Because this is non-custodial, there is no exchange hot-wallet risk in the traditional sense.
- **Smart Contract Reviews and Safeguards:** Reputable on-chain exchanges commission independent audits and run ongoing bug bounties. When using Lighter, review audit references, repository activity, and upgrade policies where disclosed.
- **Permission Boundaries:** Role-based access controls, time-locked upgrades, and transparent admin rights (if any) are critical to understand. Smart-contract governance and emergency controls should be clearly documented.
- **Transaction Hygiene:** Use hardware wallets where possible and maintain strict approval management. Revoke allowances when they’re no longer needed. If you’re new to self-custody, start with small amounts and learn **[how to store crypto](/posts/how-to-store-crypto)** securely.
- **Scam and Phishing Awareness:** On-chain interfaces are frequent targets for impersonation. Verify URLs, never share seed phrases, and learn common red flags to avoid **[crypto scams](/posts/how-to-avoid-crypto-scams)**.

Remember: On-chain transparency is a double-edged sword—auditable settlement is good, but it’s also unforgiving. Make a habit of simulating transactions where possible and double-checking approvals before signing.

## Decentralization and the Regulatory Landscape
DEXs live in a unique regulatory gray zone: the smart contracts generally run permissionlessly on public networks, but the hosted web interface you use to access them may adopt compliance controls (geo-fencing, IP filtering) and risk checks. Lighter’s availability can therefore vary by jurisdiction and product type. Perpetual futures, in particular, are often restricted in certain regions.

Traders who prioritize heavy regulatory clarity and fiat on-ramps may prefer a licensed centralized exchange in their region—US users, for example, often compare front-ends like Lighter with compliant on-ramps such as **[Coinbase](/exchanges/coinbase)**. Conversely, globally oriented traders who focus on breadth of markets and on-chain composability sometimes opt for altcoin-centric venues like **[KuCoin](/exchanges/kucoin)** (CEX) or stay fully non-custodial via DEX front-ends.

It’s crucial to understand that even if a protocol is permissionless, a public-facing web UI might impose access restrictions to reduce legal and operational risks. Advanced users sometimes interact directly with contracts when appropriate—but that moves even more responsibility to the user.

## Liquidity, Pricing, and Market Structure
For professional-grade crypto trading, the triad of liquidity depth, routing quality, and execution speed is everything. Lighter’s on-chain-first approach relies on:
- **Aggregated Liquidity:** Routing across AMMs, on-chain order books, and possibly RFQ venues to find tight spreads and minimal slippage.
- **MEV and Price Protection:** Where supported, transaction modes that mitigate frontrunning, sandwiching, and backrunning attempts, preserving quoted prices as closely as network conditions allow.
- **Latency and Finality:** Performance depends on the base chain. Faster block times and efficient mempools typically result in tighter spreads and more predictable settlement.

If you plan to trade large tickets or during volatile periods, test with partial fills and observe how price impact behaves at your preferred sizes. On-chain depth can be highly dynamic, especially for smaller pairs.

## Points to Consider
- **Gas and All-In Cost:** On-chain execution includes network gas plus protocol and LP fees. Compare your all-in cost to alternatives—especially for frequent, small trades.
- **Smart Contract Risk:** Audits reduce risk but can’t eliminate it. Use risk budgets, diversify across protocols, and consider isolating wallets for different strategies.
- **Derivatives Complexity:** If Lighter offers perps in your region, understand funding, liquidations, and leverage impact. While leverage can amplify profits, it magnifies losses.
- **Liquidity Conditions:** Depth can vary by pair and time. Thin markets widen spreads and increase slippage. Test routes and slippage limits before committing size.
- **Operational Redundancy:** Bookmark verified URLs, back up seed phrases offline, and plan for wallet recovery. If you’re not comfortable with long-term on-chain custody, review options for safer storage and when to hold funds off-exchange.
